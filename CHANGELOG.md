# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Library models without z-fighting (`GeoModelCoplanar`): faces of a ruin or home model that lie in one plane, face the same way and overlap flickered through each other as the camera moved (window frames and patches flush with their wall, slabs laid twice, a home's gable end in the plane of the body's wall). The kits had about 12 000 such pairs; the smaller of two overlapping patches (faces joined by an edge in that plane) now stands 3 cm in front of the other, a face folded over its own patch moves alone and a repeated face goes: 6 pairs are left. Faces closer than 15 mm count as one plane (decals a few millimetres off their wall flicker at a few hundred metres in a 24-bit depth buffer). The importers run it on every model they import; **Tools > Geo World > Developer > Separate coplanar faces of library models** runs it on the libraries.
- Ruins without faces back to back in one plane, and fitted buildings separated as they are built (`GeoCoplanarFaces`). The ruin materials are double-sided, so a slab whose top and underside lay in one plane flickered grey and dark through itself as the camera turned (24 of the 25 ruin models had such faces): the smaller of two faces facing opposite ways now moves 3 cm along its own normal, and each side shows its own face. Fitting brought many more faces into one plane — a ruin's bays repeated over each other at a seam, its L, T and U parts where they meet, a model squashed to its footprint, the sections of a long house with each roof overhang in the plane of the next roof — so `GeoRuinFit.Build`, a ruin of several parts and `GeoHomes.Build` now separate their fitted parts together (the 25 ruins fitted to four footprints: 16 600 overlapping pairs before, one after; about 26 ms a fitted ruin). Pairs are found in bins of normals read with their neighbours (two faces a degree apart, as a stretch tilts them, fell into neighbouring bins and were never compared: 87 of the 89 pairs left in one ruin), q is laid on p's plane before the overlap test (sloping faces a centimetre apart slid past each other when laid flat along an axis), and patches are placed from the largest down, each in front of the overlapping faces already placed, so a stack of three or four is laid out in one pass. The library models and their reduced copies are clean (`GeoModelReduction` separates the copies it makes: collapsing edges brought about forty patches back into one plane). Model instances: every second section of a row of homes is drawn 3 cm wider on each side, its roof 3 cm higher (`GeoCityBuilder.HomeInstance`), so sections that overlap at a seam no longer flicker through each other.
- The environment layer follows `GeoWorldCamera.Current` in `CraterMap`, `WarMap` and `RuinMap` too, not `Camera.main`: a world seen through a camera that renders into a texture (a drone's, the Komandor map viewer's) had no crater map, war map or ruin dust.
- Crater ponds look like water: an uneven shore (the level noised at 1.6 m and 0.5 m), a band of wet mud to the water, shallow water over a darkened bottom at the edge and silty brown-olive water further in with patches of film, wind ripples near the camera, rain rings and the drone's wash; seen low, the crater's walls cut off the sky in them. `GroundWetness.WaterAt` follows the new shore. A dry crater stays dry until the rain is well on.
- A world package made in the editor compresses its satellite imagery with the editor's DXT1 encoder at its best quality (`GeoGeneration.EditorCompressSatellite`): `Texture2D.Compress` left visible blocks seen from a drone.
- Trench ground in a build: `Resources/Terrain/WorldTerrainWetTrenches*` keep the `_TRENCH_GROUND` variants of `Game/Terrain Lit (Wet)` (with and without normal and mask maps), which a build stripped as no material used them.
- A merged city's chunks have distance detail (`GeoCityLods`, `GeoTileLoadOptions.cityLods`, on by default). A chunk copies a ruin or home model of two to three thousand triangles for every building drawn with one and had no LOD, so every triangle was drawn however far it was: Bakhmut's battlefield holds 34 M triangles in its 402 chunks, and seen along the ground from 2 km a frame drew 45 M, 20 M of them buildings (twice, with a depth-normals prepass). `LoadTile` and `LoadTileAsync` now give each chunk two coarser copies by vertex clustering on a worker thread (cells of 1 m and 3 m: about a quarter and a tenth of the triangles; the members' mean position, the first member's normal, tangent and texture coordinates as they were stored; triangles that collapse or repeat are dropped) and an LODGroup that switches at 0.3 and 0.12 of the screen height (a 220 m chunk at 60°: about 520 m and 1.3 km). Only the models' sub-meshes (2..) are clustered: the generated buildings take their colours from the palette by texture coordinate, and a merged vertex smeared them into stripes of other colours. GeoMeshSimplifier keeps outlines better, but takes seconds a chunk. Nothing is stored in the package: worlds made before this get it at load.
- Nature Renderer is found by its type (`GeoNatureRenderer.Of`), not by name: `GetComponent("NatureRenderer")` finds a script only through the project's Assets, and with Nature Renderer's DLL in this package (since 1.17) it found nothing on terrains that had one. `NatureRendererTerrain.Ensure` then added a second (refused: one per object) and threw, so in the Interceptor Drones game the grass of 9 of the 10 tiles near the camera failed (`[GroundDressing] Tile build failed`) and the takeoff point's terrain paint threw; `TileTreeSpecies`, `PackageTiles` and `GeoNatureRenderer` looked it up the same way.
- Power poles that look like poles (`GeoPowerPole`): a `power_pole` without a catalog prefab was a 25 cm square post with a bar and three cubes; it is now an СВ-95 reinforced-concrete pole as the 0.4 kV lines of Ukrainian towns and villages have them, tapered (224 to 165 mm across the line, 165 mm along it) with bevelled edges, a crossarm of 63 × 5 steel angle on a strap and four ТФ-20 porcelain pin insulators on pins with nuts (the wires are tied in their necks: `GeoPowerPole.WireTops`). One atlas drawn by `Tools~/PowerPoles/power_pole_textures.py` (`Textures/PowerPoles`, no photo sources): vibrated concrete with its bug holes and form marks, the trowelled open face, chipped edges, rain streaks, rust run down from the strap and the bolt holes, soil splashed up, lichen and the earthing wire down one face; an older look with its foot whitewashed with lime; a look scarred by shell fragments for damaged and broken poles; weathered galvanised angle with rust and bird droppings, the aluminium tie wire in the insulators' necks. A broken pole is its stump with rebar out of a jagged break and its top lying rolled onto the end of its crossarm, the wires torn off its insulators beside it. A standing pole is turned half round by its id (other faces to the street, the crossarm on the other side; the wires keep their order). About 700 triangles near (900 broken) and 60 far. A merged world draws its poles as GPU instances (`GeoInstancedMeshes`, cells of 128 m: the near shape within 100 m of a cell, the far one to 700 m) instead of copies in the prop chunks; an editable world's pole has both on an LODGroup. `GeoInstancedMeshes.Batch.near` and `far` (levels of detail; a tile file writes them as a new component kind, older tiles read as before), a template material of URP Lit with normal and mask maps (`GeoRuntimeSetup`: the poles' and trench walls' shader variant kept in a build), `GeoWarLook.Version` 2 (battlefield tiles rebuild).
- No ruderal weeds: the five weeds 1.20.0 added for abandoned ground (ragweed with fleabane, wormwood, thistles, dead stems, the low mix) are gone with their 14 entries in the default profile, `Environment/Vegetation/Generated/Ruderal` and `Tools~/TreeGen/ruderal_gen.py`. They stood as clumps up to 2-3 m across on a green mat all over yards and open ground, also away from the fighting. Abandoned ground keeps the older meadow grasses and field weeds by their `Fallow` scales; mown turf still goes there.
- Settlements after the battle (a battlefield's world: the combat bake's craters and the buildings' states; anywhere else nothing changes):
  - War map (`WarMap`, environment layer): how hard each place was hit, 0..1, in an 8192 m window around the camera at 8 m (`_WarMap`, read by LOAD by the trees and bilinearly by the ground; the crater density near its edge counts the craters beyond it; one snapshot for the worker threads) — the crater density around it (1 a hectare within ~60 m none, 15 full) or the nearest damaged building (destroyed 0.95 at its wall, heavily damaged 0.7, damaged 0.35, nothing 35 m plus half its height out), the stronger. `RuinField` keeps the loaded tiles' damaged buildings (outline, height, state, kind) for it and the layers below.
  - Tree crowns stripped by the war (`SeasonTrees.hlsl` `SeasonWarStrip`, near leaves and far cards alike, conifers too): each tree by a hash of its root takes `saturate(war × 1.6 − random)`, whole twig sprites of its atlas gone and the rest dried; the forest floor and the leaf litter under it follow (`ForestFloor`, `GroundDressing`, `WarMap.TreeStrip` on the CPU, bit for bit).
  - Dust and brick crumbs around ruins (`RuinMap`, 1024 m at 1 m, `GroundRuins.hlsl` after the roads): grey-beige cement and plaster dust over the ground, concrete and red or sand-lime brick crumbs nearer the wall, darker rubble at the foot of a destroyed one, a ragged edge; grass thinned there. No soot or burning (a finished battle).
  - Overgrown yards: verges of abandoned towns count as settlement ground and grow as abandoned ground does (tall grass and field weeds by their `Fallow` scales), not mown turf (`LandGroups`).
  - Street props after the battle (`GeoPropDamage`, after the prop plan, at generation): by the war around a prop and a crater close by (its blast throws things away from it), by a hash of its id — the same in every tile and on every rebuild. Lamps and poles lean or snap at their foot and lie with their arm on the ground, fences lean, lie flat or have gaps (cut into 2.5 m spans that share their fate in 7.5 m runs and fall to one side), walls are chipped, breached with rubble in the gap, knocked flat or down to a stump (`GeoPropBaker.Segment`), bus shelters lose their panels or their roof, benches lose their backrest or lie overturned, bins and signs are knocked over, some props are gone. Props placed from photos keep their look. `PropPlacement.damage/lean/leanToward`, `GeoPlacedProp.damage`.
  - Power lines (`GeoPowerLines`, generated and labelled so: exports have none): concrete poles (`GeoPowerPole`) 30-38 m apart on one side of the streets of houses (residential to primary, a house within 35 m), four wires a span (three phases and the neutral) hung from what the war left of them — sagging 0.5-0.9 m, up to ~3 m slack where the fighting was, snapped and hanging down from both poles, fallen with a broken pole and lying on to its top, ending on the ground toward a pole that is gone. A span belongs to its first pole's tile and reaches the next tile's pole through the planner's `beyond`. Drawn by the new `GeoWorld/Wire` shader: a ribbon facing the camera at least 1.5 pixels wide and as faint as the wire is thin (at least 18 % near), gone at 420 m, no shadows. `EnvironmentData.battlefield` (set by `GeoCombatFile.AddTo`) turns it on.
  - Worn homes (`GeoHomeWear`, `GeoWorld/Home`): library homes where the fighting was get copies of their materials on a URP Lit forward pass with a wear layer (three levels by the war around them, at least the middle one for a damaged home) — grime and dust, thick at the foot of the walls, old stains run down from the eaves, the windows of a home blown out, boarded with OSB, covered with film or only dirty (one look a home), sheets gone and cracked in a grid on the roofs. The models are not changed; the shadow, depth and meta passes are URP Lit's (the wear rides in properties URP Lit leaves unused, so the SRP Batcher still batches them).
  - Debris at the walls of ruins: a kit generated in Blender (`Tools~/TreeGen/debris_gen.py`, Cycles on the CPU; `Environment/Vegetation/Generated/Debris`): brick, concrete, slate and wood bits, brick and concrete chunks, a slate sheet, planks, a floor slab with rebar, a buckled iron sheet and a beam, three of them with collider twins (`_prop`). Profile entries with the new `FlyPointDetailEntry.NearRuins` lie only within 3-10 m of a damaged building's wall (`GroundDressing.Rubble`, per detail cell; inside any outline none, the ruin model has its own; a tile is dressed again when ruins near it arrive later with a neighbouring tile, `GroundDressing.RedressForRuins`), on hard ground and roads too (half as many on a carriageway); the takeoff point and the fly point scatter leave them to the world.
  - Roads after the battle: asphalt and paving torn around craters (`GroundRoadScars.hlsl`, by the crater map: chunks heaved and of different tone, cracks between them, some knocked out to the soil) and mud from the fields on them where the fighting was; tracks of tracked and wheeled vehicles across the ground (`GroundWar.hlsl`, procedural: up to two 80-190 m tracks in each 200 m cell, never traced from imagery; drawn after the ruin dust, not on carriageways; `_GroundTracksOff` 1 turns them off for comparison) — still faint from the air and under grass (the dressing does not thin the grass in them yet). Package tiles' ground road and crossing meshes are switched off where the environment layer draws the roads (`PackageTiles.HideGroundRoads`): they lay over them as a second surface.
- `GeoWarLook.Version` in the fingerprint of every tile and package with a combat bake: "Rebuild changed tiles" rebuilds the battlefield tiles made before these changes and leaves other worlds alone. The war of props and homes follows `applyCondition` (off: the buildings count as intact); props drawn by a catalog prefab lean or lie on the ground (a prefab wall goes or leans instead of breaking); the planner keeps the categories of the buildings already in the scene (the power lines look for houses) and plans the poles before the street decoration.

## 1.20.0

- Battlefields: an area after a finished battle (no fighting goes on: no fire, smoke or artillery), from its satellite imagery and its map. The combat bake (`GeoCombatBake`) runs once for an export, before any tile is built, and writes `combat.json` next to its `environment.json` (`GeoCombatFile`, format `geo-combat` 2): **Bake battlefield** in the Geo Tiled World's Inspector (every export of the world, then Rebuild changed tiles), `GeoWorldRequest.battlefield` (and `combat` for its settings, `GeoCombatSettings`) for world packages, or `GeoCombatBake.Run`. The file names the export it was baked from (origin and bounds): next to another export it is ignored with a warning, and extracting the area again deletes it. It is part of every tile's fingerprint and of a package's input hash; a bake that changes nothing leaves the file as it was. `ReadEnvironment` and `ReadBuildings` merge it (their new `combat` argument, true by default); `environment.json` may carry `craters[]` and `trenches[]` of its own (`CraterFeature`, `TrenchFeature`).
  - Craters seen on the imagery (`GeoCraterDetector`, read in pieces of a kilometre with a 100 m overlap at 0.49 m a pixel through `GeoSatellite.Pixels`): pits from about 2 m across up to bomb craters, with fresh ejecta and water in them. Reading data out of imagery needs a licence that allows it (Esri World Imagery's terms do not for this use); `GeoCombatSettings.detect` turns it off.
  - The small pits no imagery resolves (mortars, drones, most shells) scattered where the fighting was (`GeoCraterScatter`): per 50 m cell from the craters the imagery shows near it and the damaged buildings around it, half of them in salvo clusters; 55 % 1-1.8 m, 35 % 1.8-3 m, 10 % 3-5 m, a fifth to a third as deep as wide; most of them worn and grown over (`freshShare` 0.1). Never on a footprint (1.5 m around it) or in water, three times less often on a road; the same pits on every bake of the same area.
  - Trenches in the style the war dug them, never traced from imagery (`GeoTrenchScatter`): a tree line under fire (from 2 craters a hectare around it, with a chance growing with the density) gets a zigzag fire trench 1-3 m outside its trees on one side, dugouts into the trees every 35-60 m and one or two meandering communication trenches back through them, all abandoned for one to three years and cut where they would cross a building, water or a road. Tree lines (`GeoTreeLines`): OpenStreetMap tree rows, narrow straight woods (their long axis) and narrow strips of forest, scrub and orchard on the ground map (thinned and traced), each with how far its trees reach on either side (walked across it: an even-width strip's skeleton lies off its middle).
  - A state for buildings the app gives no evidence for (`GeoBuildingEstimates`): within 150 m of the craters, from one crater a hectare (55 % intact, 30 % damaged, 10 % heavily damaged, 5 % destroyed) to 15 and more, or where half the buildings around are damaged on the app's radar (4 % intact, 38 % destroyed); marked estimated with low confidence, by a hash of the building's id. The app's evidence always wins. In Bakhmut's centre the radar marks 1 085 of 2 763 buildings, and the rest stood whole.
- Craters and trenches in the ground (`GeoCombatGround.Carve`, after the water and the road grading): a bowl, a lip and an apron of thrown-out soil (`GeoCraterProfile`, after Glasstone & Dolan; a worn crater half filled with a lower, wider lip) and a trench groove with spoil banks (`GeoTrenchProfile`; worn: slumped walls, wider and shallower). The deepest bowl wins where they overlap and a lip inside another crater's bowl goes with it. They fade out within 3 m of building footprints and 4 m of water (lakes included). Craters under 1.5 cells of the height grid are left to the environment layer; trenches are meshes (below), the terrain cut under them. The height grid goes up to 2049 (`heightResolution`; at 1025 a 1 km tile has ~1 m cells and keeps craters from 1.5 m; at 2049 the longest step of loading a tile took ~640 ms against 110-380 ms, and a 2 x 2 km package took 186 s to build against 70 s).
- Battlefields in the environment layer:
  - `CraterField`: the world's craters and trenches, the same list the tiles were carved with, in `<world folder>/Environment/Craters` (`CratersBaker`, **Tools > Environment > Bake Craters**, and `WorldMapBaker` with the rest of the map data; where exports overlap each crater from the export that owns its ground, as the tiles take it) and in a package's world map extra. Found through `WorldMapData.Craters`.
  - `CraterMap` (installed by `WorldEnvironment`): a 256 m window around the camera at 0.25 m (`Hidden/Game/Crater Map`, BlendOp Min) with the relief of the craters too small for the height grid, every bowl, fresh ejecta and crater water; `GroundCraters.hlsl` in `Game/Terrain Lit (Wet)` bends the normals of the small ones, darkens bowls, lays fresh soil around fresh craters and fills ponds: water standing in craters the imagery shows wet all year, every bowl and trench filling in long rain (still brown water of its own, ice in winter). Crater worlds get per-pixel terrain normals (`Resources/Terrain/WorldTerrainWetCraters`), so carved craters keep their shape where the terrain's LOD smooths its vertices. `GroundWetness.WaterAt` and `WaterNear` know the ponds (rotor spray over them).
  - Craters the imagery shows are not darkened again (their shadow and wet soil are in the satellite already, and a second darkening made bomb craters black holes); the scattered pits, which it does not show, are. Trenches are not drawn into the map any more: they are meshes, and the map's groove landed on them.
  - No grass in fresh crater bowls and ponds (`GroundDressing`, the takeoff point; old ones are grown over), and none, nor props at the takeoff point, on a trench's mesh out to the end of its lip, where the terrain under it is cut lower (it would grow through the mesh); thinner on the outer slope of its banks. Where craters stand at three a hectare or more (`CraterField.AbandonedPerHectare`; the background scatter alone stays under it) the ground is abandoned: cropland grows as meadow, with no rows, tramlines, furrows or summer stubble (`FieldRows.hlsl`, `SeasonHarvest` and the field plants stay off there), and a plant entry may grow there by its own scale instead of its land group's (`FlyPointLandScales.Fallow`, on open ground: meadow, fields, yards, bare ground; 0 keeps the group's). The default profile has five ruderal weeds for it (`Environment/Vegetation/Generated/Ruderal`, built by `Tools~/TreeGen/ruderal_gen.py`, Blender, real geometry baked into card atlases): ragweed with fleabane (to 1.9 m), wormwood (1.4 m), thistles (1.7 m), last year's dead stems (above the snow in winter) and a low mix of couch grass, plantain and dry grass; the fine meadow cards thin out under them and mown turf goes.
  - Tile trees there are damaged by the war (`TileTreeSpecies`): 10 % plus 2.25 % per crater a hectare of them, from three a hectare (up to 55 %), as dead trees (35 %), snags (40 %) and windthrow (25 %).
- Destroyed buildings drawn better (`GeoBuildingDamage`): the broken top of the walls is one profile around the whole outline (`BrokenTop`) instead of a height drawn for every 2.4 m run on its own, which read as a comb of organ pipes on apartment blocks: a heavily damaged building stands roofless mostly up to its top floors, with one to three bays fallen in (6-18 m across, down to a storey or two) and a top that breaks along the storeys, its window openings dark on every floor still standing; a destroyed one is stumps of a storey or less with one to three fragments standing (a corner more often than not, 3-9 m across, up to 0.8 of the walls). Broken walls have their thickness inward from the outline (two faces, a cap, ends where the wall ends; outward, they had buried the facade's window openings), plaster or brick below and sooty above outside, burnt inside, plaster loss and bare brick in stretches of a few metres; heavily damaged buildings dusty and sooty in tone instead of a flat dark slab; rubble mounds finer and lumpier, brown-grey per triangle under the dust. Building meshes may carry per-vertex shades (`GeoBuildingMesh.shade`, and `Wall` with a shade running up it), which a merged city keeps in its palette (`GeoCityBuilder`). A destroyed 12 x 9 m building is 836 vertices (the test's budget 900, was 700 with the comb), a heavily damaged one 920; the library's ruin models have 2-5 thousand.
- Trenches in 3D (`GeoTrenchMesh`): a height grid of about a metre cannot hold a slot 1.3-1.5 m wide with steep walls, so every trench is a mesh on its tile and the terrain is only cut under its slot (`GeoCombatGround`: every node within a cell's diagonal of the slot below it by `GeoTrenchMesh.Clearance` and by how much the ground falls within a cell, so no terrain triangle rises through it; trenches narrower than the grid used to be left to the environment layer's drawing, and the terrain's own spoil banks are gone: the spoil is the mesh's). Past the slot the terrain keeps its height and the mesh lies over it, 3 cm up (`GeoTrenchMesh.Lift`); the craters are carved first, the trenches laid on that ground and their slots cut after (`GeoWorldBuild`), so a crater beside a trench shows on its spoil and its rim does not rise through it. Mesh and cut follow the centreline without its spikes (`GeoTrenchMesh.Centreline`: a point turning by more than 110° is dropped; the scatter's lines had a few short arms turning back on themselves, and a sharp inner corner's mitre reached far past them). The cross-section (`GeoTrenchSection`): a floor at the depth, walls steep as dug and slumped when old (their foot spreads into the floor, the top edge rounds over), then the spoil, a low heap from the edge up to a round crest and down to its toe, where it runs out into the ground level and flat (`Profile`, a biweight: no height and no slope at the toe), so the ground carries on without a crease; past the toe an apron at the ground level over every terrain triangle the cut lowered (`LipWidth`: two cell diagonals past the edge), then the mesh turns down under the terrain. Each side's heap (`Heap`) swells and shrinks along the trench over 5-8 m and a little over 2 m, its toe nearer or farther, its crest nearer the edge or farther out, higher and wider on one side (the parapet), with clods on it (noise placed in the world, ±4.5 cm when fresh) that die out toward the toe; eight columns across it. (The first banks were piles of 2-4 m whose height doubled from one to the next, a row of loaves, five columns across, flat facets with creases, and still stood a tenth of their height at their edge, where they broke steeply under the terrain: a crease and a ragged line along the field.) Each side is swept on its own, a cross-section every metre: round the outside of a corner a fan of cross-sections, on the inside the mitre (a point that would fold behind the corner takes the mitred cross-section's point, to the bit: each row's own point on the bisector stood apart from its neighbour's and the quads between them stood up as slivers). The ground's normals come from the faces round each point, whichever strip it belongs to (the strips' own broke along the mitre, a fan's edges and clipped junctions); the walls' texture runs along each side's own length (round a fan it streaked). A trench ends in a rounded end wall with its heaps fading round it to 15 % (the spoil lies along a trench, not round its end), the apron round it pushed out so the polygon's sides cover the cut; one that leaves another (a communication trench, a dugout) is cut at the other's floor and opens into it: it gets a cross-section where it crosses the other's wall line and only its floor before it, the other gets cross-sections where its walls are crossed, and the spoil and walls of either inside the other are cut away exactly along that wall line (triangles clipped and re-triangulated; whole triangles left in or out stood up as shards). One object per chunk under the tile's `Trenches` group, with a collider. Sub-mesh 0 is the ground (floor, spoil, apron), drawn with copies of the tile's terrain material (`GeoTrenchGround`: in play they follow the terrain's material and data, and are made again when their layer settings are lost — no properties of a terrain shader, Unity sets them on the terrain's own draws, so a shader reload in the editor dropped them from the copies and the spoil drew black, a dark band beside the lit field); a terrain of more than four layers (a GeoTerrain world without satellite has twelve) gets a pass for every four, the shader's add pass on the next control map, with the ground's triangles again in sub-meshes 2..; UV1 says how much of the ground is the trench's own soil, which a terrain shader that knows trenches draws (the environment layer's `Game/Terrain Lit (Wet)`, keyword `_TRENCH_GROUND`, `TrenchGround.hlsl`): fresh spoil a mix of pale loess loam and chernozem in patches of a metre or so with small soft clods (gone where a pixel covers more than 12 cm), grown over in patches as it ages, fading out to the terrain's own look before the toe, and the floor dark damp mud with little sky over it, all from the ground detail layers (`field_dry`, `grass_soil`, `mud`, `dark_soil`: `_TrenchSoilLayers` and `_TrenchSoilOn` from `GroundDetail`) and before wet ground, seasons and snow; elsewhere the ground is the terrain's look. Sub-mesh 1 is the walls, the wall atlas `Textures/Trenches` (`Tools~/Trenches/trench_textures.py`, from Poly Haven scans already in the package: cut soil in its horizons — turf and roots, chernozem with fine roots, yellow-brown loess with spade or bucket marks, pebbles and krotovinas, loose wet soil slumped at the foot —, boards laid flat behind posts, weathered and muddy, some broken out, and wattle; its occlusion darker toward the foot, where little sky reaches); each stretch of wall (5-11 m) takes a strip by the trench's kind: a fire trench mostly bare, boards in a quarter, wattle in some; a communication trench bare; a dugout's pit lined. The modular trench kit of FPV Defence Simulator was not used: stylised, nothing like a real trench. Packages carry the meshes as any tile mesh; the wall atlas is in the runtime registry (`GeoRuntimeSetup`). Bakhmut's centre: 103 trenches, 206 000 triangles over 4 km².
- Damaged buildings are drawn with the ruin library's models almost everywhere (`GeoRuins.Assign`): when no model of the building's kind and condition fits, the nearest does (`GeoRuinLibrary.ChooseNearest`: a condition a step off, then a related kind, then both), and an L, T or U footprint (fill under `minimumFill`) is split into the rectangles it is made of (`GeoRuins.Parts`: the largest rectangle that fits, again and again, up to ten of at least 3 m and a sixteenth of the footprint; where two meet each reaches 0.6 m into the other), each with its own model (`GeoBuilding.ruinParts`, worked out before every build; the first part's model preferred where it fits about as well). A heavily damaged or destroyed building may leave half its footprint to the parts' debris, a damaged one a quarter. Bakhmut's centre: procedural ruins 220 to 10 of 2 487, Klishchiivka's 4 to 2 of 526.
- Standing buildings no single home model fits are drawn with several (`GeoHomeLibrary.Cover`, `GeoBuilding.homeParts`): a rectangle as a row of two to four sections of one model (semi-detached houses, the sections of a block of flats, a row of garages, every other one mirrored), or one model stretched further (`LooseMinStretch`..`LooseMaxStretch`, 0.6-1.72), or an L, T or U as its rectangles (`GeoRuins.Parts`), each covered the same way; a model of the building's kind first, then of a related one (`GeoRuinLibrary.RelatedTo`). Instanced homes get an instance and a plinth for each part. Bakhmut's centre: procedural standing buildings 108 to 8 of 291, Klishchiivka's 18 to 4 of 48.
- Home models no longer stand in each other. A model's length and width were its main block's walls, and most models reach past them: 28 of the 38 have a porch or veranda 1.5 m or more in front of the walls, N12's summer kitchen runs 5.6 m past its gable, N20's garage 4.3 m, N13's wing 4.7 m (median 2.4 m). Stretched by its walls over a footprint traced from the imagery, which already holds the porch, a model reached into the next yard, onto the street and through the next house: in kharkiv_east as built, 7 340 of 18 744 homes (39 %) shared at least 0.5 m² with another, 82 % of those pairs from this alone. Now:
  - A model's plan (`GeoHomePlan`): what it covers seen from above with the roof a building gives it, from its meshes (every triangle's edges, the inside of the ones that are not upright, what the walls enclose), as its box and a few rectangles (main block, wing, porch). `GeoHomes.Measure` stretches the whole plan over the footprint's rectangle (`GeoHomes.Layout`), so the walls are smaller than the rectangle by what reaches past them.
  - Eight ways round (`GeoHomeLibrary.ChooseFit`, `GeoHomeFit`): the front to either long side or, turned a quarter (`GeoBuilding.homeQuarter`, `GeoHomePart.quarter`; `QuarterCost`), to either end, mirrored or not. The cost adds to the stretch how unlike the outline the plan is (1 - intersection / union, `ShapeCost`), so an L footprint takes an L model with its wing where the outline's is. A model's proportions change by at most 1.28 times (`MaxDistortion`; 1.5 for the loose stretch): 1.4 one way with 0.72 the other drew a house almost twice as long for its width. A plan may leave a fifth of itself outside the outline at most (`MaxOutside`).
  - Neighbours (`GeoHomeSite`, built by `GeoHomeSites.For` from every building a build is given, those around a tile too): a way round whose plan reaches more than 0.3 m² into another building's outline is out (`MaxIntoNeighbour`); the outlines decide, never the models drawn on them, so a building's choice is the same in every tile that sees it.
  - The front faces a street along the long side (`StreetFacing`), else the south (`SunFacing`: the yard side of a house with its gable to the street, where an old hata looks anyway), instead of a random side. A street is a road people live along (`GeoStreets.IsStreet`): footways, paths, cycleways and field tracks no longer turn houses to them; ruin models face the same streets.
  - An instanced home's plinth stands under its body's plan (walls, porches, wings; not the eaves), not under the footprint's whole rectangle, so the inside corner of an L and the ground beside a porch stay open.
  - On the test village (2 263 buildings, Microsoft's and mapped): homes in an overlap 935 of 2 486 (37.6 %) to 4 of 2 484 (0.2 %), pairs sharing over 20 m² 39 to 0, the worst 73 m² to 1 m².
- A yard's outbuildings are drawn as outbuildings (`GeoHomeSites.Outbuildings`, `GenerationSettings.yardOutbuildings`, on by default): the export calls a Microsoft outline or a bare building=yes a house by its size alone (35 to 300 m²), so summer kitchens, barns and garages got house models with porches to the street; in the test villages 44 % of those houses stood within 25 m of a house 1.3 times their size. A guessed house under 160 m² is drawn as a shed (`GeoHomeSites.AsOutbuilding`, a copy) when a house 1.2 times its size (and 45 m²) stands within 30 m and 3 m or more nearer the street, or, with no street within 80 m, one 1.5 times its size, or when it is under 45 m² with a house 1.6 times its size within 15 m: 28 % of them there. Mapped houses stay houses. The related models a shed or garage may borrow are never house models.
- Buildings of two exports that stand in each other on their seam (`GeoBuildingConflicts`, in `GeoTileSource.Merge`): each part is extracted and its overlaps resolved on its own, and a tile takes every building from the part that owns its centre, so a house Microsoft detected in one part and OpenStreetMap mapped in the other came twice, and one part's outline was never cut against the other's building. The extractor's pass (`conflicts.py`: OpenStreetMap over Microsoft, the larger over the smaller, half under another dropped, else cut along its wall) runs on the buildings a tile gathers from several parts; a cut building is a copy.
- A building centred on the line between two tiles is built by one of them (`GeoBounds.Owns`: the lower edges in, the upper ones out), not by both.
- `BuildingFeature.source` and `categorySource` (the export's `source`, osm or microsoft, and how the category was found).
- Ruin models are never stacked on themselves (`GeoRuinFit.Measure`): the height plan could tile a model with no storeys to repeat (the planner's fallback for rows of whole models along a footprint), and a destroyed block's model on a building twice its height hung a second copy of its rubble a storey up, under nothing (12 buildings in Bakhmut's centre). Upward a model without storeys is stretched up to 1.4 times its eaves, what is left of a destroyed building (a model only of that) stands as high as its model shows it (1.15 at most), and a model drawn lower than the building costs more to choose (`Fit.shortfall`), so taller models win; a pitched model on a flat building keeps its roof in proportion to the walls it stands on.

## 1.8.0

- Fixed: the parts of one area (Kharkiv as six exports) were cut into tiles as separate worlds, each on a grid that began at its own export's lowest corner, with full 2 km tiles at its edges. Tiles of neighbouring parts did not line up and overlapped by hundreds of metres, up to 1.9 km between the north and south groups.
- One grid for every export: tile (i, j) is the square [i x size, (i + 1) x size) x [j x size, (j + 1) x size) of the export's own coordinates, whatever its bounds are. A tile has the same index, place and name in every export. A world made before this version (its grid began at the export's corner) is rebuilt in full.
- One world from several exports: Tools > Geo World > Generate one world from several exports... (also the Several exports... button of the Geo World window, `GeoTiles.Generate(parts, ...)`, `GeoDefaults.Generate(folder)` and `-geoExport <folder of parts>` in batch mode). Parts must be exported with the same fixed origin; different origins are refused with a message.
- Who owns the border: the exports are listed in order and each owns the ground of its bounds that no earlier one owns (a rectangle: its region). Regions never overlap, so no ground is built twice. A tile is built once. In the middle of an export it is that export's alone; where exports meet it is built from all of them: each gives the ground (height), roads, rivers, buildings, trees and props inside its own region, and roads are cut at the seam between the regions. The tile records the exports it came from (Tile > Source) and its root has Tile Parts.
- A tile built from several exports is rebuilt from the Geo Tiled World object (Rebuild changed tiles / Rebuild all tiles), which now lists the exports (Parts). The tile's own Rebuild refuses it with a message.
- Tiles that no export reaches any more (an export removed or changed) are deleted with their scenes and assets; the grid no longer depends on the first export, so a part can be added later without moving any tile.
- Ground classes (the material map) and the canopy of a tile built from several exports come from the export that holds most of it. Water, areas, fences and hedges that cross the seam are drawn by both exports (twice over their overlap).
- Tests: six new (the shared grid, the owner of every point, slivers of tiles, two exports as one world, different origins) and the riverside tiled sample, which now has 4 x 2 tiles because its grid is shared (it was 3 x 2 on its own corner).

## 1.7.0

- Tile generation exports terrain tree instances and instanced tree batches to Resources/TerrainChunker/TreeData, with stable world/tile keys and asset GUIDs.
- Geo World restores external trees asynchronously in batches, releases runtime tree data on unload, and starts Nature Renderer after trees are ready.
- UniTask scene streaming supports Addressables and built-in scenes, staggered activation, retries, and cleanup when the streamer stops.
- New tiled worlds register each tile as a separate Addressables bundle. Existing worlds retain built-in streaming; enable Addressable Scenes and rebuild changed tiles to migrate.
- Requires Addressables 2.9.1 and UniTask 2.5.10. Install UniTask via its Git package URL before updating Geo World, then rebuild tiles and Addressables content. External trees are restored in Play mode and are not shown in Edit mode.
- Validated with three passing Unity tests covering tree serialization, stable asset regeneration, and repeated scene load/unload with tree restoration.
