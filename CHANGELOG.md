# Changelog

## 1.14.0

- Trees as tall as what they stand in (`GeoTreeHeights`, Tree Heights on the world's `GeoLandCover`): the generator plants its trees 5 to 10 m tall and a mapped tree without a height at 7 m, a young wood everywhere (in Kharkiv's tiles almost every tree was 7-8 m, where a mature forest is 18-25 m). When a tile's trees load in Play mode (`GeoTerrainTreeLoader`), before Nature Renderer sees them, a tree with a generated height takes one by the land cover around it: a wood 15-24 m (forest on 45 % or more of the ground within 50 m), tree rows, shelterbelts and trees among fields 8-14 m, orchards 4-7 m, single trees in villages and towns 6-12 m (built-up ground under it or on a tenth of the ground within 60 m). It takes the tree prefab whose own height is nearest (one of those within a third of the best fit, by a hash), so that its scale stays within half and twice the prefab's, and its width keeps its proportion; heights and prefabs come from a hash of the tree's place, so a tile looks the same every time it loads. A tree whose height is outside the generated range had it from the map and keeps it. Nothing is baked or rebuilt: worlds made earlier get it once their land cover is baked (1.13). It runs once a tile, on the main thread while the tile's trees load (`GeoTreeHeights.SizedMilliseconds` says how long); Nature Renderer starts after it as before. Off: Tree Heights > Enabled.
- `GeoLandCover.At(x, z)`: the world whose land cover has the tile under a point. `GeoTerrainTreeLoader.Sized`: how many of a tile's trees were sized; `GeoTreeHeights.SizedTrees` and `SizedMilliseconds` for all tiles since Play mode started.
- A tree prefab's height is measured in one place for the generator and the runtime (`GeoTreeHeights.PrefabHeight`; `GeoTerrainTrees.Height` calls it).
- Tests: the trees of a wood, a shelterbelt, an orchard, a village and the meadow next to it take their ranges, a mapped height and a tree off the land cover are kept, scales within half and twice the prefab, widths in proportion, the same heights twice; a prefab's height.

## 1.13.0

- Land cover: a tiled world knows what its ground is, for a game to ask anywhere (`GeoLandCover` on the world's object). The export's ground map (materials.png: ESA WorldCover 2021 with OpenStreetMap's land use, woods, orchards, water, roads, railways and building footprints painted over it), which only painted the terrain while generating, is baked onto the tiles' grid at 10 m: one `GeoLandCoverData` a tile in `<world folder>/Land cover`, a byte a cell and the class of each byte by name (10 KB for a 1 km tile, 4 MB for 20 x 20 km, loaded with the main scene). `GeoLandCover.TrySample(position, out GeoLandClass value)` gives the class at a point and `TryGetFractions(position, radius, fractions)` the share of each class among the cells within a radius, across the tiles' edges and whether the tiles there are loaded or not (`Share` sums some of them). `GeoLandClass` has the app's ids (materials.py): grass, cropland, forest, scrub, bare, sand, rock, wetland, water, urban, asphalt, paving, gravel, dirt road, path, rail, building, snow, orchard. A cell takes the most common class of 4 x 4 points in it (a 4 m track across a field leaves it a field), and where exports overlap the export that builds the ground there says what it is, as for the terrain.
- It is baked after tiles are generated or rebuilt, and by Bake land cover in the Geo Tiled World's Inspector for a world made earlier: a few seconds for 20 x 20 km, from the exports; no tile is rebuilt or opened, and a tile whose land cover is the same keeps its asset untouched. Nothing a tile is built from changes, so no tile is rebuilt for it. World packages (runtime generation) carry no land cover yet.
- Tests: the classes are the app's ids; the export that owns an overlap and the most common class of a cell; the data read by its class names; sampling and shares across the edge of two tiles; the riverside sample's tiles have the land cover of its ground map.

## 1.12.0

It is cut away under every loaded tile of a package (`GeoLoadedTiles`: whoever loads tiles, LoadTile and LoadTileAsync register them), and the far clip of the cameras that see it reaches its farthest corner while they render (at most 150 km), as the overview of a tiled world (1.10).

A zone generated next to another downloads only the tiles the first did not, and one background is made for all the packages of a scene (`ShowAsync` with all of them): no tile is drawn twice. Each computer makes its own from the network and its cache, a game's clients too (nothing to transfer).
- `GeoWorldGenerator` downloads a new package's background tiles into the cache before it reports the package done (stage Background; `GeoWorldRequest.background`, `backgroundSettings`); `GeoWorldBackground.PrefetchAsync(manifests, settings, progress, cancellation)` does the same for any packages, `ShowAsync(packages, progress, cancellation)` makes the background (the missing tiles downloaded first, then a few milliseconds of the main thread a frame) and `Clear` removes it. A package whose origin is not the scene's (another zone) lies at `PackageOffset(package)`.
- The runtime demo shows the background around its package.
- Tests: the rings of an area, a neighbouring zone downloading only its own tiles, one background for two packages with no tile twice and the coarse ring left out under the sharp one, the ground's elevation and curvature, the cut under a loaded tile.

## 1.11.0

- Worlds are generated without the editor, in a built player (runtime generation). `GeoWorldGenerator.GenerateAsync(request, progress, cancellation)` takes an area (south, west, north, east in degrees, up to 100 km² and 20 km a side), runs the Building Extractor app that comes with the build (a process of its own, with its own Python, progress as it goes, killed on cancel), then generates every tile with the same code as the editor's tiled worlds and writes a world package: a folder with `world.json` (format version, origin lat/lon of the export's local ENU on WGS84, bounds, tiles, terrain statistics: relief, mean slope, forest, built-up, water and open ground, buildings, roads; the SHA-256 of the inputs and of the content) and one `tiles/tile_x_z.gwt` a tile (meshes, textures, materials, terrain data, the home sets, the objects and their components, and every building's outline for navigation). The same export with the same settings gives the same bytes. `GeoWorldPackage.Load(folder)`, `LoadTileAsync(index, parent, options, cancellation)` (read and unpacked on a worker thread, made a few milliseconds of the main thread a frame) and the handle's `Unload()`; `GeoWorldPackageStreamer` streams a package around the camera.
- The generator is an assembly of its own that runs in a player (`GeoWorld.Generation`): data, terrain, roads, water, buildings, props, satellite imagery, tile cutting (the classes keep their namespace, `GeoWorld.Editor`). The editor's generation calls the same code (`GeoWorldBuild`); what only the editor does (assets, Undo, the progress bar, Crunch, prefab instances, the project's reduced models) goes through a generation target (`GeoGeneration`). A package made in the editor (Tools > Geo World > Runtime generation > Make a world package from an export...) is the one a player makes.
- A player has the package's library through `GeoRuntimeAssets` (Tools > Geo World > Runtime generation > Prepare runtime assets: Assets/GeoWorld/Resources/GeoWorldRuntimeAssets.asset): home and ruin models and their reduced copies, tree prefabs, road and ground pictures, building styles, the water material, shaders, and template materials that keep the shader variants of the generated materials in a build. A package refers to these by key (GUID and local file id) instead of carrying them. A standalone build of such a project gets the Building Extractor app in `StreamingAssets/GeoWorld/Extractor` (about 30 MB).
- No NavMesh in a package: a game builds its own for its agents. A loaded tile has a terrain collider, colliders of bridges and props, mesh colliders of its buildings' outlines (one per 200 m cell; the drawn buildings have none) and `GeoNavigationTag` markers (ground, building not walkable, water not walkable, bridge) to map to its areas; `GeoWorldTileHandle.Navigation` lists the buildings with their outlines, ground, height and condition.
- Generation in a second process of the same player (`GeoWorldGeneratorHost.GenerateAsync`): the player's own executable started headless with `-geoworld-host`, the game's scenes never wake up in it, its progress comes through a file and cancelling kills it with the extraction. In the game's own process a tile takes its main thread for the whole of its build (half a minute for a dense town tile of Lyman), so a game with a UI to keep alive (the instructor's operation editor) generates in a second process and only loads the package.
- A package's homes have their roofs tinted to the export's colours by the generation itself (in the editor the tinted roof materials are assets of the project, GeoHomeImporter); a loaded tile's building colliders are cooked on a worker thread.
- Tools > Geo World > Runtime generation > Create demo scene / Build demo player... (`GeoRuntimeDemoBuild`): a player with bounds, Generate, the progress, Cancel and the packages made, which stream around the camera.
- A player's package keeps the satellite imagery as DXT1 (Crunch needs the editor): about four times the bytes of a crunched editor world, read without decoding.
- Tests: a package of the riverside sample (manifest, the same bytes twice, a changed tile fails its hash), its tiles loaded back as the generator made them, colliders and navigation markers, unloading, the registry's keys, the extractor's arguments in any culture, the area limits.
- A package carries no overview (the world from high above, 1.10.0): it is baked by the editor for a tiled world; a package's settings keep its values.

## 1.9.5

- Water has a shader of its own (Shaders/GeoWater.shader, GeoWorld/Water, on the shared material Resources/GeoWorld/Water instead of URP Simple Lit). The colour comes from the water's depth under the surface (the camera's depth texture): light green-teal near the shore, dark in the middle, and the shore fades out softly instead of ending in a hard edge on the ground. A river's bed was carved with a narrow, steep falloff, so at the edge of its ribbon the ground was still about the whole depth below the water: in the Chuhuiv sample the edge hung in the air over the bed in all 396 sections of the Siverskyi Donets (1.5 m of air), with a dry trench between the water and the bank. A river is now shaped like a thin lake (`GeoShore`, shared with the lakes) from its distance to the edge of its water: the bed falls from the water level, the ground outside never lies lower than the water and goes back to its own height without a wall, and the river's surface goes a little under the banks, so its edge is under ground. The bed is worked out from the nearest piece of river at every node, so tiles agree at their edge.
- Fixed: rivers much narrower than the real river, and beside its middle, which made the water look missing where the satellite picture shows a wide river. OpenStreetMap draws a river as a line and the export gives it the width of its kind (14 m for any river, 2 m for a stream). The generator now reads the ground map of the export (the water class, ESA WorldCover with the OSM water polygons burned in): where water lies within 12 m of the line, the river gets that width at every sample (a median over 24 m, so a bridge or a tree does not change it; it widens by at most 0.5 m for each metre along it) and its line moves to the middle of that water. Lakes, river mouths (water 90 m or more across) and rivers narrower than the map keep the width they have. `PathBuild.widths` carries the widths into tiles; the ground under a river is painted by them too.
- Fixed: the trees of a tile that is switched off and on again (a game that culls tiles, or a scene that disables them) were gone for good where Nature Renderer draws them. The tree loader destroyed the tile's own copy of the terrain data when the tile was switched off, and Nature Renderer holds on to the data it first drew from: it came back to "the object has been destroyed" and drew nothing (MissingReferenceException from TerrainGrassRenderer in the console). Switching Nature Renderer off and on did not help, only its Restart did. The loader now keeps the tile's trees while it is only switched off and lets them go when the tile is destroyed (a tile that is unloaded, as before). No tile needs rebuilding.
- Nature Renderer's delayed start (GeoNatureRendererStart) now holds it back every time the tile is switched on, not only when it first loads, so that it never starts on the empty terrain data of the moment; it is told the terrain and its data when it starts, and restarted if the tile's trees arrive after it was already running. Frame numbers are reset with every Play mode (with Enter Play Mode without a domain reload, Nature Renderer waited for the frame count to pass the last Play mode's).
- Checked with the real Nature Renderer on a 16-tile world of 2 km tiles (340 000 trees, Lyman): flying the length of the world and back at 200 m, every tree within the terrain's tree distance (1 500 m) is drawn on every stop, on a first load and after tiles were unloaded and loaded again; Nature Renderer is drawing 3 to 34 frames after a tile loads. Beyond 1 500 m trees are not drawn by design (Terrain Tree Distance, the tree prefab's LOD cull).
- Tests: three new for the tree loader and Nature Renderer's start (a stand-in for its component, in a test-only assembly outside the Editor folders).
- The level of a river's water is the mean of the ground under its line over 40 m (it wobbled with the noise of the elevation data); the ground is carved to it.

## 1.8.1

- Fixed: a wall along the seam between exports. Each export's heightmap was cut from the elevation data for its own area, so where two exports meet their heights differ (by a little, or by a whole datum when the ground level at the origin differs) and every tile took its own export's numbers. Now the ground is one function of the point for the whole world: the exports are first brought to one datum (each is moved by the median difference to the earlier export it overlaps, over the overlap), and across the overlap the heights of the exports that reach a point are averaged, weighted by how far inside its bounds the point is plus a 24 m feather, so the ground goes over from one export to the next instead of stepping. Away from a seam a point has one export's own height. Every tile gets the same height at a point whichever export builds it. The height scale (Terrain Base Y and Range) is the world's, not each export's.
- Fixed: lakes drawn as squares with gaps against the terrain. The surface was a grid of squares laid from each tile's own corner, and a square was kept only when its four corners and its middle were inside the outline: the shore was stepped, the water stopped short of it, a pit of the full depth showed between the water and the terrain, and the squares of two tiles did not line up. Now the bed slopes from the shore (at the water level) down to the depth over a bank of 4 to 14 m, the ground just outside the shore comes down to the water level on a gentle slope, and the surface is flat quads on the terrain's own height grid (which every tile shares), one for each run of wet cells in a row: the outline is followed to the grid's resolution, the cells the outline touches are wet, and the edge of the first of them is under the bank. Surfaces of two tiles meet exactly.
- One lake from several exports: the outlines that two exports have of one lake (the same id) are one surface at one level, taken from the first export that has it, and a lake across a seam has one level on both sides.
- Tiles are rebuilt (tile version 16): Rebuild changed tiles.
- Tests: three new (the bank and the surface of a lake, two tiles' surfaces meeting, two exports with a 3 m different datum and a lake across the seam).

## 1.8.0

- Fixed: the parts of one area (Kharkiv as six exports) were cut into tiles as separate worlds, each on a grid that began at its own export's lowest corner, with full 2 km tiles at its edges. Tiles of neighbouring parts did not line up and overlapped by hundreds of metres, up to 1.9 km between the north and south groups.
- One grid for every export: tile (i, j) is the square [i x size, (i + 1) x size) x [j x size, (j + 1) x size) of the export's own coordinates, whatever its bounds are. A tile has the same index, place and name in every export. A world made before this version (its grid began at the export's corner) is rebuilt in full.
- One world from several exports: Tools > Geo World > Generate one world from several exports... (also the Several exports... button of the Geo World window, `GeoTiles.Generate(parts, ...)`, `GeoDefaults.Generate(folder)` and `-geoExport <folder of parts>` in batch mode). Parts must be exported with the same fixed origin; different origins are refused with a message.
- Who owns the border: the exports are listed in order and each owns the ground of its bounds that no earlier one owns (a rectangle: its region). Regions never overlap, so no ground is built twice. A tile is built once. In the middle of an export it is that export's alone; where exports meet it is built from all of them: each gives the ground (height), roads, rivers, buildings, trees and props inside its own region, and roads are cut at the seam between the regions. The tile records the exports it came from (Tile > Source) and its root has Tile Parts.
- A tile built from several exports is rebuilt from the Geo Tiled World object (Rebuild changed tiles / Rebuild all tiles), which now lists the exports (Parts). The tile's own Rebuild refuses it with a message.
- Tiles that no export reaches any more (an export removed or changed) are deleted with their scenes and assets; the grid no longer depends on the first export, so a part can be added later without moving any tile.
- Ground classes (the material map) and the canopy of a tile built from several exports come from the export that holds most of it. Water, areas, fences and hedges that cross the seam are drawn by both exports (twice over their overlap).
- Tests: six new (the shared grid, the owner of every point, slivers of tiles, two exports as one world, different origins) and the riverside tiled sample, which now has 4 x 2 tiles because its grid is shared (it was 3 x 2 on its own corner).
